# Course Guide to **How Brands Grow**

This literature review finds that the **How Brands Grow** literature is anchored by strong, repeatedly replicated empirical generalisations: brands usually grow through **penetration more than loyalty**, Double Jeopardy and Duplication of Purchase hold across many markets, and the Romaniuk–Sharp stream argues that **distinctiveness and mental availability** matter more than perceived differentiation, though important boundary conditions and critiques remain [[1.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-revisited-ehrenberg/3a9c570a9d8151d7b27379a25e00b574/)[[2.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-brand-performance-measures-using-ehrenberg/b4bd09a54df657a68811f2b644da09a9/)[[3.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/its-a-dirichlet-world-sharp-wright/36212be530895b0082ad9dc99b237f29/)[[4.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/evidence-concerning-the-importance-of-perceived-brand-romaniuk-sharp/df43aa3ad7045c948fc673c1e5ca0f14/)[[5.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/).

## 1. Introduction

The core question in this literature is whether brand growth is mainly explained by acquiring more buyers under regular, law-like market structures, or by building unusually loyal, differentiated customer bases. Across the strongest foundational and replication studies, the answer is usually the former: smaller brands have fewer buyers and somewhat lower loyalty, brands share customers in line with penetration, and market share differences are driven much more by buyer numbers than by loyalty differences [[1.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-revisited-ehrenberg/3a9c570a9d8151d7b27379a25e00b574/)[[1.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-revisited-ehrenberg/3a9c570a9d8151d7b27379a25e00b574/)[[1.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-revisited-ehrenberg/3a9c570a9d8151d7b27379a25e00b574/)[[6.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/purchase-loyalty-is-polarised-into-either-repertoire-or-sharp-wright/3fb0be7551775c45b52e1f33174d92c0/)[[7.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-model-of-multibrand-buying-ehrenberg-goodhardt/b8197feb4da951cb98ada2e8b7a1552e/)[[2.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-brand-performance-measures-using-ehrenberg/b4bd09a54df657a68811f2b644da09a9/).

That foundation then extends into the Ehrenberg-Bass account of brand building: growth comes from raising penetration, especially among light and non-buyers, while communications should build mental availability and distinctive recognition cues rather than rely on sharply differentiated positioning claims [[8.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/where-is-the-brand-growth-potential-an-examination-of-buyer-trinh-dawes/22d3fe1b3d8e5323a57acf9f3f017c17/)[[8.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/where-is-the-brand-growth-potential-an-examination-of-buyer-trinh-dawes/22d3fe1b3d8e5323a57acf9f3f017c17/)[[5.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/)[[5.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/)[[9.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/brand-attributes-–-‘distribution-outlets’-in-the-mind-romaniuk/cb9e56626d48582f8051d03a32003513/)[[4.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/evidence-concerning-the-importance-of-perceived-brand-romaniuk-sharp/df43aa3ad7045c948fc673c1e5ca0f14/). The strongest recent additions concern distinctive brand assets, mental market share, targeting and reach, plus new replications in digital, service, luxury, nonprofit and industrial settings that mostly support the same broad structure while clarifying deviations and limits [[10.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/modeling-mental-market-share-romaniuk/ee94acb3638c5d43ad5ca132cee84a45/)[[11.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/achieving-reach-in-a-multimedia-environment-romaniuk-beal/0a1151826adf5907bc4e34b3c24e28ee/)[[12.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/applying-marketing-laws-to-social-media-crosscultural-klepek-cui/ee0a4ad66b2c509184c6f5b3c9867e46/)[[13.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/using-the-dirichlet-model-to-predict-how-fashion-brands-chowdhury-barker/5487acb847d654c087a2de95dac3968f/)[[14.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/an-empirical-study-of-industrial-consumer-buying-bennett/8a45e6a7af815383ba4dbcfa5a151b98/)[[15.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/new-versus-frequent-donors-exploring-the-behaviour-of-the-faulkner-romaniuk/caf4d25f6dc25a9b945822681afabd16/)[[16.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-consumer-perceptions-of-luxury-brands-romaniuk-huang/da3da04affff5c84973e81bd0291d8d2/).

### 3.2 Origins and Foundations

Double Jeopardy began outside packaged goods: McPhee first identified the pattern in mass-behavior settings such as readership and listening, and later marketing studies showed the same regularity in consumer buying [[18.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/using-choice-experiments-to-find-double-jeopardy-patterns-greenacre-tanusondjaja/3c392245ffcf5a3590c0b796d73d2a44/)[[12.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/applying-marketing-laws-to-social-media-crosscultural-klepek-cui/ee0a4ad66b2c509184c6f5b3c9867e46/)[[15.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/new-versus-frequent-donors-exploring-the-behaviour-of-the-faulkner-romaniuk/caf4d25f6dc25a9b945822681afabd16/)[[19.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-theory-of-double-jeopardy-martin/6d738172d4295a2f9ec6a5c83f64d403/). Early stochastic work then formalized repeat buying and multi-brand purchase structure, showing that duplicated buying and repeat rates could be described by simple general relationships linked to penetration and market share [[20.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/progress-on-a-simplified-model-of-stationary-purchasing-chatfield-ehrenberg/76f95db252db57a2a5a348476ea0387b/)[[21.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/results-concerning-brand-choice-chatfield-goodhardt/4e2564ccfc1a511eafe5ada3625d4a3d/)[[7.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-model-of-multibrand-buying-ehrenberg-goodhardt/b8197feb4da951cb98ada2e8b7a1552e/)[[7.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-model-of-multibrand-buying-ehrenberg-goodhardt/b8197feb4da951cb98ada2e8b7a1552e/).

The 1984 Dirichlet paper consolidated this stream into a general model for stationary, largely unsegmented repeat-purchase markets, requiring only a few inputs while predicting penetration, repeat buying, SCR, sole loyalty, and duplication patterns across many categories [[17.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-dirichlet-a-comprehensive-model-of-buying-behaviour-goodhardt-ehrenberg/baeb98aa2c8c552b97789f5b72fd100c/)[[17.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-dirichlet-a-comprehensive-model-of-buying-behaviour-goodhardt-ehrenberg/baeb98aa2c8c552b97789f5b72fd100c/)[[17.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-dirichlet-a-comprehensive-model-of-buying-behaviour-goodhardt-ehrenberg/baeb98aa2c8c552b97789f5b72fd100c/)[[6.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/purchase-loyalty-is-polarised-into-either-repertoire-or-sharp-wright/3fb0be7551775c45b52e1f33174d92c0/). Ehrenberg’s later syntheses translated that model into managerially usable norms: large brands are mainly larger because they have more buyers, while loyalty differences are usually modest and systematically constrained by market structure [[2.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-brand-performance-measures-using-ehrenberg/b4bd09a54df657a68811f2b644da09a9/)[[2.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-brand-performance-measures-using-ehrenberg/b4bd09a54df657a68811f2b644da09a9/)[[2.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-brand-performance-measures-using-ehrenberg/b4bd09a54df657a68811f2b644da09a9/)[[1.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-revisited-ehrenberg/3a9c570a9d8151d7b27379a25e00b574/).

### 3.3 Replications and Deviations

Replications are unusually extensive for a marketing generalisation. Graham et al. report 32 differentiated replications and over 500 brand predictions, finding Double Jeopardy in every replication they examined, including long-run, capital-goods, aggregation, and emerging-market conditions [[22.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-–-50-years-on-reviving-a-forgotten-tool-that-graham-bennett/821f83619c4954f4b194f7cc55a052b4/)[[22.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-–-50-years-on-reviving-a-forgotten-tool-that-graham-bennett/821f83619c4954f4b194f7cc55a052b4/)[[22.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-–-50-years-on-reviving-a-forgotten-tool-that-graham-bennett/821f83619c4954f4b194f7cc55a052b4/)[[22.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-–-50-years-on-reviving-a-forgotten-tool-that-graham-bennett/821f83619c4954f4b194f7cc55a052b4/). ### 3.4 Distinctiveness, Salience, and Reach

Romaniuk and Sharp redefine brand salience as a brand’s propensity to be noticed or come to mind in buying situations, distinct from simple awareness or attitude [[5.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/)[[5.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/). Retrieval is probabilistic, linked to prior response levels, and behaves in ways consistent with NBD-Dirichlet-style thinking about memory accessibility [[5.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/)[[5.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/)[[5.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/). Romaniuk’s mental market share paper extends this by showing that associative network size and structure can be benchmarked with Dirichlet logic and fit observed data well [[10.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/modeling-mental-market-share-romaniuk/ee94acb3638c5d43ad5ca132cee84a45/)[[10.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/modeling-mental-market-share-romaniuk/ee94acb3638c5d43ad5ca132cee84a45/).

The distinctiveness argument is that brands usually compete with limited perceived differentiation, so growth depends more on being easily identified and mentally available than on owning unique valued propositions [[4.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/evidence-concerning-the-importance-of-perceived-brand-romaniuk-sharp/df43aa3ad7045c948fc673c1e5ca0f14/)[[4.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/evidence-concerning-the-importance-of-perceived-brand-romaniuk-sharp/df43aa3ad7045c948fc673c1e5ca0f14/)[[4.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/evidence-concerning-the-importance-of-perceived-brand-romaniuk-sharp/df43aa3ad7045c948fc673c1e5ca0f14/)[[29.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/differentiation-or-salience-ehrenberg-barnard/eda671f13f415f42ab2ff16e4771fd38/)[[29.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/differentiation-or-salience-ehrenberg-barnard/eda671f13f415f42ab2ff16e4771fd38/). Image attributes act like “distribution outlets” in memory, and brands associated with more category cues are more likely to be bought later [[9.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/brand-attributes-–-‘distribution-outlets’-in-the-mind-romaniuk/cb9e56626d48582f8051d03a32003513/)[[9.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/brand-attributes-–-‘distribution-outlets’-in-the-mind-romaniuk/cb9e56626d48582f8051d03a32003513/)[[9.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/brand-attributes-–-‘distribution-outlets’-in-the-mind-romaniuk/cb9e56626d48582f8051d03a32003513/). Reach evidence points the same way: most headroom lies in light or non-buyers, and additional media touchpoints often add duplicate rather than new audience, so broad efficient reach matters more than narrow loyalty targeting [[8.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/where-is-the-brand-growth-potential-an-examination-of-buyer-trinh-dawes/22d3fe1b3d8e5323a57acf9f3f017c17/)[[8.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/where-is-the-brand-growth-potential-an-examination-of-buyer-trinh-dawes/22d3fe1b3d8e5323a57acf9f3f017c17/)[[11.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/achieving-reach-in-a-multimedia-environment-romaniuk-beal/0a1151826adf5907bc4e34b3c24e28ee/)[[11.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/achieving-reach-in-a-multimedia-environment-romaniuk-beal/0a1151826adf5907bc4e34b3c24e28ee/)[[30.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/quantifying-the-target-market-for-advertisers-graham-kennedy/602c6d589b2554f280488bc53443e664/)[[30.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/quantifying-the-target-market-for-advertisers-graham-kennedy/602c6d589b2554f280488bc53443e664/).

### Results Timeline

Brand-growth research has moved from behavioral regularities to memory-based branding applications.




Early work established stochastic buying laws and the foundations of Double Jeopardy and Duplication of Purchase [[7.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-model-of-multibrand-buying-ehrenberg-goodhardt/b8197feb4da951cb98ada2e8b7a1552e/)[[17.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-dirichlet-a-comprehensive-model-of-buying-behaviour-goodhardt-ehrenberg/baeb98aa2c8c552b97789f5b72fd100c/). Based on this corpus, Byron Sharp, Jenni Romaniuk, and Andrew Ehrenberg appear most often, with Australasian Marketing Journal, Journal of Business Research, and Journal of Advertising Research as especially visible outlets [[3.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/its-a-dirichlet-world-sharp-wright/36212be530895b0082ad9dc99b237f29/)[[5.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/)[[1.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-revisited-ehrenberg/3a9c570a9d8151d7b27379a25e00b574/).




## 4. Discussion

The strongest part of this literature is replication breadth. Double Jeopardy, Duplication of Purchase, and the broader Dirichlet framework recur across categories, countries, and time periods, including stores, services, industrial buying, nonprofit contexts, e-commerce, and some digital media settings [[1.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-revisited-ehrenberg/3a9c570a9d8151d7b27379a25e00b574/)[[2.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-brand-performance-measures-using-ehrenberg/b4bd09a54df657a68811f2b644da09a9/)[[14.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/an-empirical-study-of-industrial-consumer-buying-bennett/8a45e6a7af815383ba4dbcfa5a151b98/)[[15.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/new-versus-frequent-donors-exploring-the-behaviour-of-the-faulkner-romaniuk/caf4d25f6dc25a9b945822681afabd16/)[[13.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/using-the-dirichlet-model-to-predict-how-fashion-brands-chowdhury-barker/5487acb847d654c087a2de95dac3968f/)[[12.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/applying-marketing-laws-to-social-media-crosscultural-klepek-cui/ee0a4ad66b2c509184c6f5b3c9867e46/). That replication record makes the penetration-over-loyalty claim unusually strong for marketing science [[22.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-–-50-years-on-reviving-a-forgotten-tool-that-graham-bennett/821f83619c4954f4b194f7cc55a052b4/)[[3.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/its-a-dirichlet-world-sharp-wright/36212be530895b0082ad9dc99b237f29/)[[8.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/where-is-the-brand-growth-potential-an-examination-of-buyer-trinh-dawes/22d3fe1b3d8e5323a57acf9f3f017c17/).

The weaker part is causal interpretation. Many studies are descriptive or benchmarking exercises, not experiments, so they establish regularities and useful norms more clearly than they isolate mechanisms [[24.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/making-sense-of-common-dirichlet-deviations-scriven-bound/27e83e3790de5af8a5f2d770c32859aa/)[[33.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/learning-from-data-an-empiricsfirst-approach-to-relevant-golder-dekimpe/f49957e0c72b5eafb621b7d6b9d2e6a4/)[[33.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/learning-from-data-an-empiricsfirst-approach-to-relevant-golder-dekimpe/f49957e0c72b5eafb621b7d6b9d2e6a4/). Distinctiveness and mental availability are conceptually rich and managerially influential, but direct evidence on Category Entry Points and specific asset classes is thinner in this corpus than the broader salience literature, and some newer asset work is very recent [[5.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/)[[34.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-natural-monopoly-effect-in-brand-image-associations-stocchi-pare/c51a328474b35bb3ababcc47ff54ebf0/)[[35.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/shapebased-assets-are-strongest-benchmarking-phua-bali/463899242b9d51f8bc79556dff39d75a/).

Critiques matter but usually modify rather than overturn the framework. Brand equity, satisfaction, store-brand structure, luxury meaning, and online contexts can produce excess or deficit loyalty, steeper slopes, or partitions, yet these tend to be deviations around stable norms rather than wholesale failures of the laws [[36.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/brand-equity-or-double-jeopardy-chaudhuri/6711a50e7c82561594c898282a186f72/)[[37.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/is-your-brands-loyalty-too-much-too-little-or-just-right-bhattacharya/eabdfb7145c25a30ac2ccad6a3b4722d/)[[38.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/factors-that-influence-manufacturer-and-store-brand-dawes/107dddd662875ec7a4a6f19f4d4f7e95/)[[39.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/examining-the-brandlevel-associations-between-customer-dawes/86cc6c8fe873583195d24301325fef32/)[[16.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-consumer-perceptions-of-luxury-brands-romaniuk-huang/da3da04affff5c84973e81bd0291d8d2/)[[40.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/a-comparison-of-online-and-offline-consumer-brand-loyalty-danaher-wilson/df8b0807bced531b8db939dd18bc5186/)[[24.6]](https://consensus.app/papers/making-sense-of-common-dirichlet-deviations-scriven-bound/27e83e3790de5af8a5f2d770c32859aa/).

## 5. Conclusion

For a university course guide, the safest synthesis is that **How Brands Grow** rests on a strong behavioral core and a somewhat less settled cognitive-extension layer. The behavioral core—Double Jeopardy, Duplication of Purchase, Natural Monopoly, and Dirichlet benchmarking—is one of the most heavily replicated bodies of empirical generalisation in marketing [[2.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/understanding-brand-performance-measures-using-ehrenberg/b4bd09a54df657a68811f2b644da09a9/)[[3.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/its-a-dirichlet-world-sharp-wright/36212be530895b0082ad9dc99b237f29/)[[34.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-natural-monopoly-effect-in-brand-image-associations-stocchi-pare/c51a328474b35bb3ababcc47ff54ebf0/). The cognitive-extension layer—mental availability, brand salience, distinctive assets, and reach-first strategy—is influential and increasingly supported, especially through Romaniuk’s work, but still has clearer evidence for broad principles than for every specific managerial tactic [[5.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/conceptualizing-and-measuring-brand-salience-romaniuk-sharp/1e04004427dd5fa3947cbe0b461f4cee/)[[10.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/modeling-mental-market-share-romaniuk/ee94acb3638c5d43ad5ca132cee84a45/)[[42.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/assessing-branding-strength-comparing-marketer-brus-hartnett/4f4da2a5fbb2563a940c4a0804496639/).

### Research Gaps

The corpus is rich on repeat-buying regularities and increasingly good on mental availability, but thinner on direct tests of Category Entry Points, asset execution choices, and causal mechanisms linking memory structures to long-run market-share change.



The clearest gap is that distinctive-asset research is growing faster than its causal validation base. There are useful benchmark and recognition studies, but fewer long-run or experimental demonstrations tying asset strength changes to market performance shifts [[35.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/shapebased-assets-are-strongest-benchmarking-phua-bali/463899242b9d51f8bc79556dff39d75a/)[[42.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/assessing-branding-strength-comparing-marketer-brus-hartnett/4f4da2a5fbb2563a940c4a0804496639/)[[41.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-recall-and-recognition-of-sonic-logos-silas-robinson/27721bc1d7585ff0950d2980e80c1e47/). Category-cue and mental-availability work also remains more developed at the conceptual and benchmarking level than at the level of intervention studies, despite strong theory linking broader cue coverage to future purchase and lower defection [[9.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/brand-attributes-–-‘distribution-outlets’-in-the-mind-romaniuk/cb9e56626d48582f8051d03a32003513/)[[9.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/brand-attributes-–-‘distribution-outlets’-in-the-mind-romaniuk/cb9e56626d48582f8051d03a32003513/)[[43.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/brand-salience-and-customer-defection-in-subscription-romaniuk-sharp/3758b3f12f1f5734aa3c061bb0847a91/). Digital replications are expanding quickly, but they still qualify the laws by unit of analysis, genre, and market maturity, so this remains an active frontier rather than a closed case [[26.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-in-digital-entertainment-a-preregistered-šulik/35a90c5bcbc254fa83efd2fa2ace05ef/)[[25.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/double-jeopardy-in-digital-entertainment-a-replication-šulik/7519ecf309965446b6a73f11e823281a/)[[12.4]](https://consensus.app/papers/applying-marketing-laws-to-social-media-crosscultural-klepek-cui/ee0a4ad66b2c509184c6f5b3c9867e46/).

### Open Research Questions

Future work should test not just whether these laws fit, but when interventions can shift brands above or below their expected norms.

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
|  | Current evidence shows measurement promise, but long-run causal links to share growth remain under-tested. |
|  | The theory is strong, but comparative intervention evidence for specific CEP-building tactics is still limited. |
|  | Recent digital studies support the laws unevenly, suggesting boundary conditions tied to platform structure and repeatability. |

The literature therefore supports teaching Double Jeopardy, Duplication of Purchase, and distinctiveness as core course concepts, while teaching their deviations and open causal questions alongside them.
